# Security Analysis

Evidence for this section comes from five automated test suites (`npm test`, 48/48 passing; the 24 adversarial cases are tagged `[adversarial]` and run on their own with `npm run test:adversarial`). Every requirement that the paper's Section "Security and Privacy Requirements" places in scope is exercised by at least one adversarial or lifecycle test below, not just asserted in prose. The three it places out of scope (secure transmission, emergency access, and anonymization) are not tested.

| Requirement (paper Section 3) | Test | Result |
|---|---|---|
| Access control — only the identity owner may transfer ownership | `test/security.test.js`: *"an attacker cannot transfer ownership of an identity they do not control"* | PASS — reverts with `EHRRegistry: not authorized`; owner unchanged |
| Access control — only the identity owner may authorize a delegate | `test/security.test.js`: *"an attacker cannot grant themselves as a delegate on another identity"* | PASS — reverts; `validDelegate` stays `false` |
| Access control — only the identity owner may revoke a delegate | `test/security.test.js`: *"an attacker cannot revoke a legitimate delegate on another identity"* | PASS — reverts; the legitimate delegate's authorization is left intact (attacker cannot even harm it) |
| Access control — only the identity owner may publish/revoke attributes (service endpoints, document hashes) | `test/security.test.js`: *"an attacker cannot publish or revoke attributes on another identity"* | PASS — both the publication and the revocation of an owner-published endpoint revert |
| Patient consent has a natural expiry (delegated authorization is time-bounded, not indefinite) | `test/security.test.js`: *"a delegate's authorization expires on its own after the granted validity period"* | PASS — `validDelegate` flips to `false` once the granted period elapses, with no further transaction required |
| Verifiable, immediate revocation (explicit revocation does not wait for natural expiry) | `test/security.test.js`: *"revoking a delegate takes effect immediately"*; `EHRRegistry.test.js`: *"revokeAttribute emits validTo = 0"* | PASS |
| Audit control — every change to an identity's state is recorded on-chain | `test/security.test.js`: *"every ownership/delegate/attribute change is independently auditable via on-chain events"* | PASS — a delegate change, an attribute change, and an ownership change each emit their event with the new value and the block of the identity's previous change, so the whole history can be walked back from the latest change. Events do not carry the prior state, and only writes are audited: who *read* a document is not recorded anywhere |
| Data integrity of off-chain (IPFS) documents | `test/offchain.test.js`: *"returns the original bytes when a document is fetched by its CID and decrypted"*, *"refuses to decrypt a ciphertext altered after encryption"*, *"refuses to decrypt a document with any key but the one it was encrypted under"*, *"gives an altered document a different CID"* | PASS — the decrypted document is byte-identical to the original; AES-GCM rejects an altered ciphertext or a wrong key; an altered blob gets a different CID, so it cannot be passed off as the anchored one |
| Credential integrity — a VC cannot be altered after issuance without detection | `test/vc.test.js`: *"rejects a VC whose payload was tampered with after signing"* | PASS — signature verification fails (`invalid_signature`) |
| Credential integrity — an expired VC is not accepted | `test/vc.test.js`: *"rejects a VC that has already expired"* | PASS |
| Credential verifiability after a key change (limitation, documented) | `test/vc.test.js`: *"no longer verifies a credential signed before the issuer's DID changed owner"* | Confirms the limitation: after `changeOwner` (or a recovery), a credential signed with the previous key fails with `invalid_signature`, because verifiers resolve the issuer's current DID Document |
| Issuer authenticity — a VC's issuer cannot be spoofed by writing another DID into the payload | `test/vc.test.js`: *"cannot be forged by putting the doctor's DID in the payload and signing with a different key"* | PASS — verification attributes the credential to whoever actually signed it, not whoever the payload claims |
| Key management — private key storage | `test/offchain.test.js`: *"stores the key only in encrypted form and recovers it with the right password"*, *"rejects a wrong password"* | PASS — the V3 keystore file (geth/MetaMask format) does not contain the raw key, decrypts back to the same account with the right password, and refuses a wrong one |
| Key management — recovery from genuine key loss (not merely revocation/delegation, which are covered above) | `test/recovery.test.js`, `scripts/demo-guardian-recovery.js` | PASS — a pre-registered M-of-N guardian set (17 tests) can jointly move a lost identity to a new address without the old key; a single guardian cannot act alone or double-vote; approvals from removed guardians, from before a recovery, or cancelled by the owner do not count; a new owner does not inherit the previous owner's guardians; the old key is provably powerless immediately after recovery |

## Interpretation

The registry governs who may change an identity's state, and that check is enforced entirely on-chain by the `onlyOwner` modifier in `EHRRegistry.sol` — there is no off-chain gatekeeper to bypass. It does not govern who may read a document: that depends on who holds the document's AES key, which is distributed out of band, so enforcing read access is left to that key distribution and to the systems that rely on the registry. Delegates have no on-chain power either: a valid delegate appears in the DID Document's `authentication` list, so a relying party can accept a JWT it signs on the identity's behalf, but it cannot call any registry function. Every delegation carries an expiry (`addDelegate` always takes a validity period, though the contract does not cap its length), so a forgotten revocation degrades to "no access" rather than "permanent access," which is the safer failure mode. Verifiable Credentials add a second, independent integrity layer on top of the registry: even if a VC is copied and passed around off-chain, tampering or forgery is cryptographically detectable without needing to touch the blockchain at all.

Key management — previously an open gap — is now addressed by two complementary mechanisms: (1) at-rest storage via a standard, password-encrypted keystore file, so the raw private key is never persisted in the clear; and (2) recovery from genuine, total key loss via a guardian-based social recovery extension to `EHRRegistry.sol` (`setGuardians`/`approveRecovery`), modeled directly on the same trust-delegation concept already used for use case (iv). Recovery clears the guardian set on execution, so the new owner must deliberately re-authorize guardians rather than silently inheriting a potentially stale or compromised set. Approvals are counted per recovery epoch, which moves on with every change of guardians or owner and on `cancelRecovery`; an earlier version did not do this, so a removed guardian's approval, or one left over from before a recovery, still counted, and with threshold 2 a single new approval could complete a recovery.

## Known limitations (not covered by this PoC)

- No formal verification, static analysis, or third-party audit of `EHRRegistry.sol` was performed; the contract implements the unsigned half of the widely used ERC-1056 interface, but that is not a substitute for an audit. The recovery-epoch flaw above was found in review, not by the tests.
- Denial-of-service / gas-griefing resistance was not tested. The only loops in the contract iterate over an identity's guardian list (`setGuardians` checks for duplicates, `isGuardian` scans the list), whose length is chosen by the identity's own owner, so a large list can only make that owner's own recovery more expensive; this was not adversarially probed.
- Guardian recovery has no time-lock: recovery executes the instant the threshold is met. `cancelRecovery` lets an owner who still holds the key discard approvals before then, but a real deployment would want a delay after the threshold is met, during which the legitimate owner could still cancel, to defend against a majority of guardians colluding or being compromised. Against key theft the mechanism does not help: a thief holding the key can change the guardians or the owner first.
- A transfer of ownership or a recovery leaves the previous owner's delegates and attributes in place until they expire or the new owner revokes them; after a hospital's acquisition, its former delegates could still authenticate as it. `test/EHRRegistry.test.js` confirms the behaviour and the revocation. The contract cannot drop them itself, since resolvers rebuild the DID Document from delegation events.
- Recovering the DID does not recover the AES keys of the patient's documents, and Verifiable Credentials signed by an identity's previous key no longer verify against its current DID Document once its owner changes.
- Cancelling is all-or-nothing: `cancelRecovery` discards every pending approval, and a guardian cannot withdraw only its own.
